# Stock Market Fundamentals: One-Page Summary

**Week 2 | FinTech Domain Fundamentals | Prepared by RAGHUNATHAREDDY GR**  
**Purpose:** Domain education only, not investment or trading advice.

A **stock market** is a regulated marketplace where investors buy and sell securities. A company may raise capital by issuing shares in the primary market; existing shares are then traded by investors in the secondary market. In India, the **National Stock Exchange (NSE)** and **BSE Limited** are major exchanges. The **Nifty 50** is a benchmark index tracking 50 large, liquid NSE-listed companies; the **BSE Sensex** tracks 30 selected companies listed on BSE. Index movement summarizes a segment of the market, but it does not describe every listed company.

An **IPO (Initial Public Offering)** is a company's first public issue/listing process. An **SME IPO** is an offering by an eligible small or medium enterprise through the relevant SME platform and applicable rules. **Market capitalization** = current share price × shares outstanding; it measures market value of equity, not enterprise value or intrinsic value.

Key valuation and shareholder terms:

- **EPS (Earnings Per Share)** = profit attributable to ordinary shareholders ÷ weighted-average ordinary shares. It is an accounting profit measure per share.
- **P/E ratio** = share price ÷ EPS. It compares market price with earnings per share and must be interpreted alongside growth, risk, and industry norms.
- **P/B ratio** = share price ÷ book value per share. It compares market value of equity with accounting net assets, but is less informative for some asset-light businesses.
- **Dividend** is a distribution to shareholders if declared by the company. It is not guaranteed.
- **Bonus issue** distributes additional shares to existing holders from eligible reserves; **stock split** divides each share into more shares with a lower face value per share. Neither action by itself creates economic value just by increasing the share count.
- **Trading volume** is the number of shares traded over a period. It measures activity, not whether the stock is a good investment.

Financial statements answer different questions: the **Profit and Loss (P&L) statement** reports performance across a period; the **Balance Sheet** reports assets, liabilities and equity at a date; the **Cash Flow Statement** reports cash movements from operating, investing and financing activities. Analysts commonly review revenue growth, operating margin, net profit margin, current ratio, debt-to-equity, return on equity (ROE), EPS and operating cash flow. Ratio definitions must be applied consistently when comparing companies.

Market plumbing matters too. A **brokerage platform** routes customer orders through a registered intermediary and exchange; a **demat account** holds securities electronically through a Depository Participant (DP). **NSDL** and **CDSL** are India's depositories. Under the standard equity rolling settlement process described by Zerodha Varsity, settlement is generally **T+1** (trade day plus one settlement day), subject to exchange calendar, instrument and applicable rules. **SEBI** is the market regulator. A typical lifecycle is: KYC/account setup → funds/securities availability checks → order → exchange matching → clearing corporation obligation → securities and funds settlement → broker contract note and customer ledger update.

**Analyst mindset:** validate reporting period, accounting basis, units, corporate actions, trading calendar, and data freshness before calculating ratios. A high P/E or unusually high volume is a signal to investigate, not a conclusion.
